## The pattern

Moats are what make a product defensible. Common moat types:

- **Network effects** — more users = more value (each new user benefits everyone)
- **Data moat** — accumulated data competitors can't replicate (years of usage data)
- **Switching costs** — once integrated, hard to leave
- **Brand / trust** — reputation that takes years to build
- **Distribution** — access to users competitors can't reach
- **Operational excellence** — better unit economics from accumulated learning
- **Regulatory / legal** — barriers to entry

Most solo-built / vibe-built products have NO moat — they're easy to copy. That's not a fatal flaw, but it's important to KNOW. No-moat products compete on execution speed; moat-bearing products can be slower and still win long-term.

For any product: give an honest assessment. Some have a data moat, some have switching costs, some have a brand or distribution advantage. Most have no real moat yet, and that is fine as long as it is named.

## Response

1. **Surface the question:** "Moat for `<product>`? What stops a competitor from copying it in a weekend?"
2. **Walk the moat types:** check each — does this product have it?
   - Network effects?
   - Data moat?
   - Switching costs?
   - Brand / trust?
   - Distribution?
   - Operational excellence?
   - Regulatory / legal?
3. **Honest answer:**
   - Real moat: explain it specifically
   - No moat (yet): name it; ask "can a moat be built?" or "is this a short-arc play?"
4. **Yield.**

## Suppression

- Pre-product (no moat to assess yet)
- Personal-use only (moat irrelevant if there's no market)
- Mode is `explore`

## Override

- "no-moat acknowledged — short-arc play"
- "moat-building plan exists — log and proceed"
