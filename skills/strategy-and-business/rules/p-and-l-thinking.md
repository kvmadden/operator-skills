## The pattern

Every project has a P&L:

- **Costs** — time spent, money spent, attention used, opportunity cost (what didn't get done)
- **Returns** — use (how often it gets used), learning (what you learned), money (revenue or savings), identity (alignment with who you want to be)

Honest P&L distinguishes projects that earn from projects that look-productive-but-don't-earn. Parallel-build patterns especially benefit from this clarity.

Without explicit P&L, every project feels equally worthy of continued investment. With P&L, the picture sharpens: some projects are net-positive (use exceeds cost), some are net-zero (use = cost), some are net-negative (cost exceeds use). Continuing net-negative projects is a tax on the net-positive ones.

With several projects competing for attention, P&L thinking keeps all of them from getting equal attention by default. The under-earning ones become candidates for `kill-criteria`.

## Response

1. **Surface the lens:** "What's the P&L on `<project>`?"
2. **Walk through:**
   - **Costs:** time spent so far, time projected, opportunity cost (what's not getting done)
   - **Returns:** actual use, learning, money (real or projected), identity-fit
3. **Honest evaluation:**
   - Net-positive: continue with awareness
   - Net-zero: explicit decision (continue as investment OR adjust)
   - Net-negative: `kill-criteria` territory
4. **Yield.**

## Suppression

- Mode is `explore` — exploration is by-design un-P&L'd
- Project is too new to evaluate (give it time)
- Project's value is genuinely qualitative (creative, identity) — but even then, name the costs honestly

## Override

- "qualitative value confirmed — log as identity investment"
- "too early — revisit in <timeframe>"
