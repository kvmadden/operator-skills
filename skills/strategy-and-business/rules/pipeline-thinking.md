## The pattern

Every key role has a pipeline question:

- Who could do this role in 12 months?
- What's between them and ready?
- What's the development plan to close the gap?

Reactive staffing (only thinking about a role when it opens) produces:

- Scramble hiring (lower quality)
- Long vacancies (operational pain)
- Talent loss (high-potential people leave when they don't see development)

Pipeline thinking is the discipline that distinguishes teams that scale from teams that keep filling vacancies. It's invisible work — you don't see the pipeline payoff until 6-18 months later. That's why most people skip it.

The same question applies to any key role or component with a single owner:

- The operator's own role: who could cover it if the operator were out?
- Roles the operator depends on: who is developing into them?
- Open-source or vendor dependencies with one maintainer: what is the fallback?

## Response

1. **Surface the question:** "Pipeline for `<role>`?"
2. **Run the pipeline check:**
   - "Who could do this role in 12 months?"
   - "What's between them and ready (skills / exposure / authority)?"
   - "What's a concrete development move toward ready?"
3. **If no candidate identified, that's the problem to solve first** — talent surfacing before development
4. **Yield.**

## Suppression

- Genuinely new role with no clear pipeline (still worth naming; might inform recruiting)
- Pre-position (operator doesn't have authority yet; pipeline thinking is still useful for influence)
- Mode is `explore`

## Override

- "no pipeline candidate yet — recruiting required"
- "intentionally point-in-time for this role"
