title: Taste as discipline — trainable, not innate
trigger: creative-craft decision treating taste as innate / fixed / unexaminable — "I just like X", "that doesn't feel right" without further interrogation
priority: low

## The pattern

Taste = trained recognition of what works + why. Components:

- **Exposure** — wide and deliberate intake across modes / eras / contexts
- **Critique** — naming what works, what doesn't, why; not just "I like / don't like"
- **Vocabulary** — having words for what you're seeing (rhythm, restraint, contrast, density, voice)
- **Pattern recognition** — spotting what makes great work great across instances

Untrained taste:

- Defaults to comfort (more of what you already know)
- Can't articulate why something works
- Confuses "I like" with "this is good"
- Stagnates

Trained taste:

- Engages honestly with work outside your comfort zone
- Articulates the craft moves
- Distinguishes "I like" from "this is good" (those overlap but aren't identical)
- Continues developing

The "innate taste" framing protects mediocrity — if taste is fixed, there's no work to do. Treating taste as trainable opens the work. The rate-of-improvement on taste determines the ceiling on every craft-bearing project.

## Response

1. **Surface:** "Taste-trained or gut-only on `<decision>`?"
2. **Walk the test:**
   - "Can you name 2-3 influences for this choice?"
   - "Can you articulate WHY this works (not just IF you like it)?"
   - "Vocabulary check — what words describe what you're going for?"
3. **If gut-only:** spend 10 min in deliberate exposure / critique before deciding — look at related work; name what works in those examples.
4. **Yield.**

## Suppression

- Acute decision (no time for taste-development; just pick)
- Domain you genuinely don't care about — taste investment isn't free; allocate it
- Personal preference that's not craft-relevant
- Mode is `explore`

## Override

- "acute — picking now"
- "low-stakes preference — not craft"
- "explore"
