## The pattern

Most ideas have prior art. Building without checking means re-deriving lessons others have already learned + missing alternatives you'd prefer + sometimes building something already-built-better-elsewhere.

The check:
1. **Quick web search** — does this exist? Open-source, indie, commercial?
2. **If yes:** Study them. What did they get right? Wrong? Why does your version improve?
3. **If no:** Why not? Either (a) genuine gap = opportunity, or (b) market signal that nobody wants this.

Building without prior-art check means re-walking known paths. 30 minutes of search saves weeks of re-derivation. A parallel-build pattern especially benefits from knowing what exists before adding to the pile.

## Response

1. **Surface the question:** "Prior art? Has someone built this?"
2. **Suggest a quick check:**
   - WebSearch the concept
   - Check Indie Hackers / Product Hunt for similar
   - Check open-source registries (GitHub, npm, etc.)
3. **Two outcomes:**
   - **Exists:** "Study them. What's their take? What does yours improve?"
   - **Doesn't:** "Why? Genuine gap or signal?"
4. **Yield.**

## Suppression

- Already checked prior art
- Mode is `explore`
- Building for personal-only use (reinventing for fun is fine)

## Override

- "already checked — nothing comparable"
- "I know — deliberate re-derivation for learning"
- "personal use only — prior art not relevant"
