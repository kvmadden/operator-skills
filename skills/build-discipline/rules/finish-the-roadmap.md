## The pattern

When the operator asks "what should I work on" with energy to do something, the right answer is usually "finish what's closest to shipping" — not "start the new shiny thing." High-energy windows are where finishing happens; without them, finishing keeps deferring and the v0.x pile grows.

The discipline: read your project list, rank unshipped items by proximity-to-ship, surface the top candidate with a concrete next-step. High-energy windows match the cognitive load of finishing — perfectly.

This is the constructive counterpart to "not-another-product" and the natural complement to v1-vs-v2 thinking. The pattern is invisible per-session ("just one new exploration") but visible in aggregate (every session opens something new; nothing closes).

## Response

1. **Pull the data:** read the project list or backlog; rank unshipped items by proximity-to-ship.
2. **Surface the top 1-2:**
   ```
   Unshipped, ranked by proximity-to-ship:
   1. <Product A>: <where it stands; concrete next step>
   2. <Product B>: <same>
   
   Recommendation: <A>. Reason: <closest to actually shipping; high-energy window matches the load>.
   ```
3. **Yield to the operator's choice.**

## Suppression

- Operator already named the one-thing for the session
- Energy is low (don't push hard work at low energy)
- Mode is `explore` — exploration shouldn't be redirected to finishing
- All products / projects are shipped (nothing to finish)

## Override

- "I want to start fresh — new energy, new thing"
- "the unshipped pile isn't where my energy is"
- "explore"
