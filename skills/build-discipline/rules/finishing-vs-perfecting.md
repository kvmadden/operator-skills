## The pattern

Finishing > perfecting. Two states:

**Finishing:** "This is the best within time / scope I committed." Acknowledges flaws; ships anyway. Lessons go into the next piece.

**Perfecting:** "Just one more pass." Each pass produces marginal improvement that's mostly invisible. Piece never ships. Lessons don't transfer.

Rule of thumb (Pareto): most of the value comes from a minority of the effort.

## Response

1. **Surface:** "Finishing-mode or perfecting-mode on `<piece>`?"
2. **Diagnostic:**
   - "What were you going to fix on this pass?"
   - "Is anyone but you going to notice the difference?"
   - "What's the bar you set when you started?"
3. **If perfecting:** ship as-is.
4. **If genuine finishing:** name what you're doing; commit to pass-count limit.
5. **Yield.**

## Suppression

- High-stakes one-shot piece (commercial release, major event)
- Pre-skilled work that genuinely needs more passes
- Discovered structural problem mid-finish (fix-mode, not perfect-mode)
- Mode is `explore`
