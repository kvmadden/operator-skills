## The pattern

Each direction calls for different formality, deference, directness, and assumed-context. Off-status framing produces real damage — too-casual-up reads as "doesn't know their place"; too-formal-down reads as cold / distant; over-deferential to peers reads as insecure.

Status-line framing matrix:

| Direction | Formality | Deference | Directness | Assumed context |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Up (senior stakeholder) | Professional | Moderate | Direct (if they like it) | High — they know your work |
| Up (executive) | Higher formality | Higher deference | Strategic-direct | Moderate — higher altitude |
| Peer (cohort) | Casual-pro | Low | Collaborative | High — shared context |
| Down (junior colleagues) | Pro-warm | Low (you're senior) | Direct + supportive | Lower — explain context |
| Lateral (cross-functional) | Pro-warm | Equal-respect | Direct + people-aware | High — they see the whole org |
| Vendor | Pro | Equal | Relationship-investing | Low — they need you to anchor |

## Response

1. **Name the direction:** "Drafting `<up | peer | down | lateral>` to `<audience>`."
2. **Surface the matrix expectation:** "Expected: formality `<X>`, deference `<Y>`, directness `<Z>`, assumed context `<W>`."
3. **Compare to current draft:** "Current draft reads `<formality | deference | directness | context-level>` — match? If no, suggest rephrase."
4. **Yield.**

## Suppression

- Already status-tuned this draft
- Mode is `explore`
- Audience-status relationship is established and consistent

## Override

- "intentional off-status (informal up, etc.)"
- "audience knows me well — relaxed framing OK"
