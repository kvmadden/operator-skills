## The pattern

When the operator signals a difficult upcoming conversation — performance talk, escalation, conflict, hard feedback — the conversation lands better with 5-10 minutes of structured prep than with none. The operator tends to walk in with a topic but no named outcome, no drafted opening, and no anticipation of the other person's likely reactions, which is how hard conversations derail.

## Response

1. **Surface:** "Hard conversation with <person> about <topic>. Five minutes of prep makes this land instead of derail — let's structure it."
2. **Walk the structure:**
   ```
   Outcome wanted: <specific — what success looks like>

   Opening (first 30s):
   "<Acknowledgment>. <Reason for conversation>. <Tone-setting>."

   Body — specific behavior, impact, request:
   - Behavior: <what they did>
   - Impact: <what happened as a result>
   - Request: <what changes>

   Hold space for — likely reactions:
   - <Reaction A>: respond by <X>
   - <Reaction B>: respond by <Y>

   Avoid: reactivity if they push back, "but" apologies, fixing when they need a witness, lecturing.

   Exit:
   - Wrap by: <agreed next step, or "I'll think on what you said">
   - Follow-up: <when, how>
   ```
3. **Yield.** Never script the whole conversation, force phrasings, or lecture on hard-conversation theory — the operator's in-the-moment responsiveness does the rest.

## Suppression

- Already prepped, or the conversation is happening immediately with no prep time
- Conversation is explicitly informal ("no prep needed")
- Mode is `explore`
