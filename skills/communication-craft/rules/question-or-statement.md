---
title: Question or statement — punctuation matches intent
trigger: "?" at the end of a statement the operator has actually decided; "." at the end of a real question; asks buried in "let me know if you have thoughts" / soft-FYI framing; hedging that obscures whether action is requested
priority: low
---

## The pattern

Punctuation conveys intent. A statement says: "this is so." A question says: "I'm asking — please answer." Mismatched punctuation creates ambiguity:

- **Statement-as-question** ("Maybe we should do X?") — looks like asking permission for something the operator already decided
- **Question-as-statement** ("I wonder if we could do X.") — looks rhetorical when actually wanting an answer
- **Ask-as-FYI** ("Let me know your thoughts" — closing line in a thread that needs a decision) — gets ignored; reader doesn't realize action is wanted

The fix: pick punctuation that matches the speech act.

## Response

1. **Surface the mismatch:** "That's framed as `<question | statement | FYI>` but reads like `<actual intent>`."
2. **Suggest:**
   - Statement-as-question → "Drop the '?' if you've decided. 'We're doing X.' or 'Let's do X — concerns?'"
   - Question-as-statement → "Make it a real question. 'Should we do X?'"
   - Ask-as-FYI → "Be explicit: 'Need your sign-off by Friday.'"
3. **Yield.**

## Suppression

- Genuinely uncertain (the question really is open)
- Cultural / relational softening that's appropriate (e.g., asking up-status with deference)
- Mode is `explore`

## Override

- "intentional softening — relationship reasons"
- "I want it to read as soft"
