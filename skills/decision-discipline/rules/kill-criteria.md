## The pattern

Every product / major initiative gets kill criteria upfront. Defined in advance, the abandonment is a clean call ("we hit the criterion — pause"). Defined at the moment of struggle, the abandonment feels like failure ("we should have made this work").

Good kill criteria are:
- **Specific** — "if no users in 6 months" not "if it's not working"
- **Time-bounded** — there's a deadline, not just a vibe
- **Observable** — the operator can tell when they're hit, not "when it feels right"

Examples:
- "abandon if no target users adopt after 3 months of offering"
- "abandon if I can't ship a working Phase 1 prototype in 8 weeks"
- "abandon if the prototype doesn't produce a sense of 'this could be a real thing' within 2 months of focused work"

## Response

1. **Surface the question:** "Starting `<product>`. What would make you abandon it?"
2. **Frame three angles:**
   - **Time:** "If no progress signal in `<duration>`?"
   - **Adoption:** "If no `<user / customer / collaborator>` engages?"
   - **Energy:** "If you're not pulled to work on it after `<period>`?"
3. **Help draft:** Suggest 1-3 candidate criteria based on what kind of product this is.
4. **Once named:** flow through `decision-log` to record as a `decision`-type entry with the criteria included.

## Suppression

- Existing project, just iterating (no new commitment being made)
- Mode is `explore` — sketches don't need kill criteria
- Sub-feature of an existing product (the parent has criteria)
- The operator pre-named the criteria: "kill if no users in X months"

## Override

- "no kill criteria — this is permanent"
- "I'll figure them out as I go"
- "internal-use-only, no abandonment risk"

On override, log + yield. The override pattern is data — a sustained "no kill criteria" tendency is worth naming at the next review.
