## The pattern

Insights surface in conversation and then evaporate; decisions get made and re-litigated months later because nobody captured the rationale. A "pin" is the antidote: a short, durable entry in a project notes file (for example `NOTES.md`) that future sessions will read.

Pin-worthy content is **durable** (still relevant in a week or month), **reference-shaped** (the operator will want to look it up), and **atomic** (one concept, not a whole conversation). Candidates: decisions (with rationale + alternatives), working-principle insights, commitments (timeline + deliverable), durable facts/specs, and quotes worth keeping (from the operator or someone else). NOT pin-worthy: mid-iteration thoughts, chat-shaped exploration, or anything already durable in code/docs.

## Response

1. **Name what's pin-worthy:** "That's a [decision | insight | commitment | fact] worth pinning."
2. **Compose a short title** (under 80 chars, noun phrase) + a 1–3 sentence body capturing what + why.
3. **Confirm:** "Pin as: [title]. OK to crystallize?"
4. **On confirm:** append it to the notes file under a Pinned heading. If it's a decision, chain into `decision-discipline`.
5. **Yield.**

## Suppression

- 3 pins already fired this session (back off — pin-fatigue)
- The insight is mid-thought (let it land first)
- `solo-run` mode with high-velocity execution
- Already captured elsewhere

## Override

- "no, just discussing — not a pin"
- "different title: X"
- "skip — already captured"

## Why this rule earns its keep

Pins beat hoping you'll remember, beat burying it in a chat that gets archived, and beat a notes doc you will lose. Captured once, recalled when it matters, bonded to the session that produced it.
