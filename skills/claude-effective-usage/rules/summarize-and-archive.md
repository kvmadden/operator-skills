## The pattern

Long chats accumulate decisions and conclusions that get lost when the thread sits open past usable length, or the next session starts fresh without them. At the right moment — resolved AND starting to drift — capture the durable decisions and key insights as entries in a project notes file, THEN archive the chat (out of the active list, still recoverable). Best of both: durable conclusions + a clean active surface.

## When to fire

All of: 30+ turns, the core question resolved (decision made / code shipped / plan agreed), AND drift starting OR a natural pause OR a hint at starting fresh. NOT mid-task, not under 30 turns, not a series of distinct quick questions with no big resolved thread.

## Response

1. **Name the moment:** "This thread resolved its core question (X) over N turns."
2. **Suggest the structured close:** "Want me to capture the durable decisions/insights to a notes file, then archive?"
3. **Offer the artifacts:** a 3–5 sentence summary, decision entries, key-insight entries, and the chat reference itself.
4. **Yield** — the operator decides what gets crystallized and when to archive.

## Suppression

- Mid-task (still actively working the core question)
- Under 30 turns
- Operator wants the thread kept open as reference material

## Override

- "stay open — I'll come back to this"
- "just summarize, don't crystallize"
- "skip — small enough"

## Why this rule earns its keep

Without it, decisions made deep in a chat get lost and the active list fills with noise. With it, durable artifacts get captured and the surface stays clean. Never auto-archive or auto-delete — the operator confirms.
